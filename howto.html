Planning a night of observations

The basics, start to finish. For everything this page leaves out, see the README.

  1. Set your location and date

    Type a city (or lat,lon) and pick the night you're observing, at the top of the planner. Everything below updates to match - rise/set times, favorites, the plan, all of it.

  2. Find objects worth seeing

    Browse the Messier catalog, search by name or catalog ID, or switch to the full deep-sky or Solar System catalog. Use the filters bar to narrow things down - "Visible now," "Peaks above" a given altitude, an "Apex between" window, or an "Up between" window all help cut a huge catalog down to what's actually worth pointing at that night.

  3. Save your favorites

    Click the star on any object's card to add it to your Saved Objects tab, so you don't have to re-search for it later.

  4. Check the Plan tab

    It lays out every favorite that's up that night as a timeline - each gets its own row, as a bar spanning when it's naturally above the horizon, with an altitude graph showing how high it actually climbs.

  5. Fine-tune your session

    Drag either edge of a bar to narrow or shift when you'll actually observe that object. The sunset/sunrise/twilight lines running through the timeline show which part of its window falls in full darkness.

  6. On a planetarium? Preview it on the dome

    If this page is running on a Digistar 7 system, each object's detail panel (and the Saved Objects tab, for all your favorites at once) gains Show on dome/Add all to dome buttons, so you can preview the night's objects on the dome before the real session.
